## 4. Abstract

Transportation and logistics systems are becoming increasingly connected, dynamic, and resource constrained. Their operation gives rise to large-scale optimization problems involving conflicting objectives, uncertain demand, time-dependent networks, heterogeneous fleets, human behaviour, and strict operational constraints. Classical exact and deterministic approaches remain essential, but often struggle to provide timely, adaptable solutions as problem structure, data, and operating conditions evolve.

Evolutionary computation offers a particularly strong foundation for this setting. Population-based search, multi-objective optimization, co-evolution, quality-diversity, evolutionary learning, and hybrid evolutionary methods can discover robust trade-offs and adapt decisions in simulation-rich or black-box environments. At the same time, transport applications provide demanding real-world testbeds that can drive methodological advances in constraint handling, dynamic optimization, expensive optimization, scalability, uncertainty, and explainability.

The proposed special session, Evolutionary Computation for Sustainable Transportation and Logistics (ECSTL), will bring together researchers and practitioners developing evolutionary methods for mobility, freight, infrastructure, and logistics. It welcomes methodological contributions grounded in transport challenges as well as application studies that offer transferable insights for evolutionary computation. Topics include routing and scheduling; traffic signal and network control; public transport, shared mobility, and demand-responsive transport; fleet composition and charging; last-mile and urban logistics; infrastructure maintenance and roadworks planning; autonomous and connected mobility; multimodal network design; and digital twins and simulation-based optimization.

Particular emphasis will be placed on multi-objective formulations that jointly address efficiency, emissions, safety, accessibility, equity, resilience, and cost; optimization under uncertainty and disruption; online and dynamic decision-making; surrogate-assisted and data-driven evolutionary optimization; memetic and matheuristic hybrids; and reproducible benchmarks. The session aims to connect the evolutionary computation, operations research, and transport communities, stimulate reusable methods and datasets, and establish a focused forum for evolutionary optimization in next-generation transportation and logistics.
